# Evidence Synthesis

Turn a collection of sources into a transparent, qualified synthesis.

## Claim Structure

- Identify one root conclusion for the response.
- Decompose it into independently checkable subclaims.
- Make the dependency between subclaims explicit.
- Avoid circular reasoning and unsupported inferential jumps.
- Keep each paragraph or section focused on one main argumentative function where practical.

## Evidence Matrix

For substantial research tasks, create an evidence matrix with these fields:

- Claim or subclaim.
- Supporting source and source type.
- Exact passage, data point, page, section, or other evidence location.
- Methodological or contextual limitation.
- Agreement or disagreement with other sources.
- Confidence and remaining uncertainty.

## Synthesis Rules

- Compare agreement, disagreement, methodology, recency, and possible conflicts of interest.
- Separate source findings from the assistant's synthesis.
- Do not claim consensus from a small, old, or homogeneous source set.
- Label extrapolations as interpretive or speculative.
- Explain what evidence would change the conclusion.
- Preserve important nuance instead of collapsing disagreement into a single answer.

## Output Contract

Return a concise synthesis followed, when useful, by:

- The evidence matrix or a compact version of it.
- Areas of agreement and disagreement.
- Confidence assessment.
- Evidence gaps and recommended follow-up research.
